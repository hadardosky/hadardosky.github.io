Get Up, Bruh. Time to Work Out

When people think about video games, they usually picture someone sitting on a couch with a controller in their hands. And honestly, fair enough. But what if playing a video game actually got you off that couch? That's where exergames come in.

Exergames are video games that involve physical movement or exercise. Think games like Wii Fit, Just Dance, or anything that has you waving your arms around and trying not to hit the furniture. Instead of just pressing buttons, players have to move their bodies to play. These games can offer both physical and psychological benefits. They can make exercise feel more entertaining, which matters because let's be so for real for a second, not everyone is excited about working out.

Making Exercise a Little Less Miserable

One of the biggest advantages of exergames is that they can make physical activity more enjoyable. And that enjoyment can make a difference when it comes to sticking with an exercise routine. If someone actually likes the activity they're doing, they're more likely to keep coming back to it.

Halbrook et al. (2019) discuss a study involving people with multiple sclerosis who participated in an exergame-based exercise program. After six months, 75% of participants were still following the exergame program, compared with only 20% of those doing conventional exercise (p. 1100). That's a pretty noticeable difference.

Of course, this doesn't mean playing Just Dance automatically replaces going to the gym or that every exergame is going to give you the same results. The review also discusses research suggesting that combining exergames with traditional exercise may offer the greatest physical well-being benefits.

The takeaway? Exercise doesn't have to look the same for everyone. For some people, turning movement into a game might make being active feel a lot more manageable. And if a video game gets someone moving when they otherwise wouldn't, that's a win.
