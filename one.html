Can Gaming Bring People Together?

For many people, gaming is not just something they do alone. Multiplayer games give friends, family members, and even strangers a way to interact. Players can work together, communicate, and build relationships while playing. For people who live far apart or have difficulty participating in face-to-face activities, online games can also provide an opportunity to connect with others.

Researchers Halbrook, O'Donnell, and Msetfi (2019) examine how these social experiences can contribute to well-being. Their review suggests that the way people interact during games matters. Gaming with others can create positive social experiences, especially when players cooperate instead of focusing only on beating one another.

Teamwork Makes the Game Work

Cooperative gaming happens when two or more players work on the same team toward a shared goal. Think of teammates completing objectives, protecting one another, or coordinating their actions to win a match. Instead of competing against each other, players succeed by working together.

According to Halbrook et al. (2019), cooperative games can encourage positive interactions and prosocial behavior. Prosocial behavior simply means actions intended to help or benefit other people. In a game, that might look like helping a teammate, sharing resources, or protecting another player.

The researchers also discuss how cooperative play can produce more positive interactions and lower aggression than competitive play. This does not mean every multiplayer game automatically creates friendships. The social experience depends on how players treat one another and what motivates them to play.
