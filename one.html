Can Gaming Bring People Together?

Being so real for a second, gaming isn't always a solo activity. Whether it's playing with friends, joining a random lobby, or spending hours trying to finish a mission together, games give people plenty of opportunities to interact. For some, online gaming is even a way to stay connected with people they don't get to see in person very often.

But can those interactions actually benefit us? WEEEEEEELL, the research says so, as Halbrook, O'Donnell, and Msetfi (2019) suggest that they can. Their review looks at how the social side of gaming can contribute to well-being, especially when players are working together rather than constantly competing.

Teamwork Makes the Game Work

Cooperative gaming is pretty much what it sounds like: players working together toward the same goal. This could mean protecting teammates, sharing resources, completing objectives, or coordinating strategies to win. Instead of trying to take each other down, everyone has to do their part.

According to Halbrook et al. (2019), this kind of gameplay can encourage positive interactions and prosocial behavior. In simpler terms, prosocial behavior means doing things that help other people. In a game, that might look like reviving a teammate, sharing supplies, or protecting someone during a match.

The review also discusses research suggesting that cooperative games can lead to more positive interactions and less aggression than competitive games. Of course, that doesn't mean every game session is magically going to turn everyone into best friends. How people play, interact, and approach the game still matters.
